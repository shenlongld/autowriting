Title: Let Green Development Become the Color of Our Common Future

Distinguished guests, ladies and gentlemen, friends,

When humanity looks back across the long arc of civilization, the measure of progress has never been only the height of our buildings, the speed of our machines, or the volume of our wealth. The true measure is whether our rivers still run clear, whether our land still gives life, whether our skies still open above us, and whether our development can stand before the judgment of generations yet unborn.

Today, when we speak of China's ecological progress, we are not speaking merely of statistics on a page. We are not speaking merely of a single project, a single province, or a single policy. We are speaking of a profound choice made by an ancient civilization in the age of modernity. We are speaking of how a developing nation of more than 1.4 billion people seeks growth without abandoning responsibility, prosperity without exhausting nature, and modernization without losing sight of humanity's shared destiny.

Let us turn our eyes to Qinghai, in western China, on the northeastern edge of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau. There, snow mountains rise and glaciers stretch across the horizon. There, the Yangtze River, the Yellow River, and the Lancang River begin their journeys. There, at the source of mighty waters, lies what is known as China's "Water Tower." A single drop of water born from ice and snow may travel through valleys, plains, cities, and villages, nourishing lives far beyond the plateau. To protect Qinghai is to protect China's water arteries. To protect Qinghai is to strengthen an ecological security barrier of Asia. To protect Qinghai is to write a chapter of green development on the roof of the world.

Qinghai was once known for three natural strengths: good water, good sunlight, and good wind. Good water is the gift of the river sources. Good sunlight is the gift of the plateau sky. Good wind is the gift of the vast open land. Yet resources do not become well-being by themselves. Natural advantages do not become human progress by accident. Only when a region respects nature, follows nature, and protects nature; only when a country places ecological responsibility within the wider task of development; only then can water, sunlight, and wind become engines of renewal, pillars of prosperity, and contributions to the common good.

Today, Qinghai is moving from these three natural strengths to three green contributions: green water, green electricity, and green computing power.

We see green water becoming more abundant. The protection and restoration of Sanjiangyuan, the source region of the three great rivers, has advanced steadily. China's first national park system pilot began there. Comprehensive protection of mountains, rivers, forests, farmland, lakes, grasslands, and deserts has taken root there. Land that once faced the risks of shrinking lakes, degraded grasslands, and interrupted river sources is regaining vitality. Rivers that once required anxious protection are again carrying clear water downstream. Today, the Sanjiangyuan region steadily improves its water conservation capacity and sends roughly 60 to 90 billion cubic meters of source water to downstream areas every year. The main streams of the Yellow River, the Yangtze River, and the Lancang River in Qinghai now maintain water quality at Grade II or above. This is not only water of nature. It is water of responsibility, water of livelihood, and water of the future.

We see ecological protection becoming the action of the people. Qinghai has established a river and lake chief system covering provincial, municipal, county, township, and village levels. More than 3,500 rivers and over 500 lakes have their dedicated guardians. In Sanjiangyuan National Park, more than 17,000 herders serve as certified ecological conservation workers. Across the province, more than 140,000 public-service ecological stewardship positions have been created. People who protect the environment are not left behind by development. They are respected by it, supported by it, and empowered through it. The idea that "those who protect the environment should benefit from it" is no longer an abstract principle; it is becoming a living social contract.

We see green electricity surging across the land. Qinghai has abundant water, rich sunlight, strong wind, and vast space. These are ideal conditions for clean energy. In Gonghe County, the Talatan area was once a barren land of sand and wind. Today, it has become one of the world's largest photovoltaic power parks. Across 350 square kilometers, solar panels stretch like a blue ocean. Above the panels, sunlight becomes electricity. Beneath the panels, grass has returned. Among the grass, sheep graze once again. This model of "generating power above and grazing below" is more than an industrial arrangement. It is ecological restoration, energy transformation, and income growth moving in the same direction.

We see the benefits of green development becoming tangible, visible, and shared. Clean energy now accounts for more than 90 percent of Qinghai's installed power capacity. Qinghai's green electricity reaches 16 provincial-level regions across China. The Qinghai-Henan ultra-high-voltage direct-current transmission project stretches 1,587 kilometers, carrying clean power from the plateau to central China. Through this line, western wind and sunlight illuminate eastern homes and factories. Through this line, a local resource advantage becomes part of a national development strategy. Through this line, electricity is not merely transmitted; a new path of energy transformation is made visible.

We see green computing power rising. In the digital age, computing power is a new productive force, and electricity is its lifeblood. If computing power depends on high-carbon energy, the digital future will carry an ecological burden. If computing power is driven by green electricity, technological progress can move in harmony with a low-carbon future. Qinghai is turning its advantage in green electricity into an advantage in green computing. It is exploring integrated systems that connect energy sources, power grids, industrial demand, energy storage, and computing capacity. It is advancing coordination between electricity and computing. In Qinghai, watts are becoming bits, cables are becoming data channels, and green energy is helping power the future of digital China.

These changes answer a central question: what tangible progress have China's environmental initiatives brought about? The answer is written in rivers, grasslands, power grids, and industrial chains. Waters are clearer. Grasslands are greener. Wildlife is returning. Energy is cleaner. Industries are lower-carbon. Development is becoming more resilient. People have more opportunities, regions have wider space for growth, and the nation is making a more concrete contribution to the planet.

China's ecological progress is not a story of stopping development in order to protect nature. It is not a story of sacrificing people's livelihoods for mountains and rivers. It is a story of protection through development, development through transformation, and transformation through innovation. Short-term gains can be tempting, but long-term responsibility requires discipline. Immediate growth may improve today's numbers, but ecological damage can become tomorrow's debt. Truly visionary development must measure today's achievements against the needs of the future.

Qinghai's practice tells us that balancing short-term development with long-term sustainability depends not on slogans, but on institutions, choices, and action. It depends on ecological red lines that cannot be crossed. It depends on national parks that protect the community of life. It depends on river and lake chiefs who carry real responsibility. It depends on clean energy bases that reshape industrial structures. It depends on green computing that opens new space for advanced productive forces. What must be protected must be protected firmly. What must be restored must be restored patiently. What must be transformed must be transformed decisively. What must be innovated must be innovated courageously.

This balance is also reflected in the courage to act and the wisdom to refrain. The Muli coalfield once brought short-term fiscal gains, but ecological damage offered a grave warning. Not every resource should be exhausted. Not every form of growth should be pursued. Not every increase in speed leads to the future. Closing mines, restoring ecosystems, developing ecological animal husbandry and tourism - these choices show the courage to leave behind an old path, the determination to choose a new one, and the wisdom to turn ecological value into lasting development strength.

This balance is reflected, above all, in the relationship between nature and the people. Ecological protection cannot endure if it remains only a grand narrative. It must enter daily life. It must improve livelihoods. It must make the people participants, builders, and beneficiaries. In Qinghai, herders have become ecological stewards. Photovoltaic parks have helped restore grasslands. Cold-water fish farming, eco-tourism, organic agriculture and animal husbandry, and green computing have created new sources of growth. Green development therefore has warmth. It has strength. It has a foundation in the lives of the people.

Ladies and gentlemen, friends,

China's green practice belongs to China, but its meaning reaches beyond China. Climate change respects no borders. Ecological crises leave no nation untouched. Green transformation cannot be achieved by isolated islands of action. No country can repair the Earth alone. No people can secure its future apart from the destiny of humanity as a whole. What we need is not accusation, but cooperation. What we need is not division, but partnership. What we need is not a green transition reserved for a few, but green technology, green finance, and green capacity shared more widely across the world.

When Qinghai sends green water downstream, green electricity across the country, and green computing power into the digital future, it offers more than a provincial example. It offers a lesson for our time. Ecological protection can become the foundation of development. Natural endowments can become engines of innovation. Regional practice can serve national progress and global responsibility. Developing countries can pursue modernization along a green path. Less-developed regions can find their own position in new productive forces. Human society can discover new prosperity by respecting nature rather than conquering it.

We must remember: the Earth is not merely an inheritance from our ancestors; it is a trust borrowed from our children. The duty of our generation is not only to leave behind more wealth, but to leave behind cleaner water, a more stable climate, healthier ecosystems, and industries that can endure. Let forests continue to breathe. Let rivers continue to flow. Let sunlight and wind become forces of civilization. Let technological progress serve harmony between humanity and nature.

Let us look at green development with broader vision. Let us protect our shared home with firmer action. Let us measure the achievements of our time with the deeper standard of history. Let every river become a witness to the continuity of civilization. Let every grassland become a symbol of life's renewal. Let every innovation become a step toward a sustainable future.

History is watching us. The future is calling us. Let us act together, so that green becomes the color of development, responsibility becomes the language of action, and hope becomes the common inheritance of all humanity.
